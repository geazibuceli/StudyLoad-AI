# Research References

The following publications contextualize StudyBalance AI's research theme. They do not validate this repository's synthetic dataset, model artifact, counterfactual explanations, interface, or recommendations.

## Student stress factors and machine learning

De Filippis, R., and Foysal, A. A. (2024). “Comprehensive analysis of stress factors affecting students: a machine learning approach.” _Discover Artificial Intelligence_, 4, Article 62. [https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-024-00169-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-024-00169-6)

This study motivates attention to multiple factors and interpretable feature analysis. StudyBalance AI deliberately narrows its inputs to academic planner data and does not reproduce the study or infer psychological or physiological states.

## Academic stressors and predictive modeling

Bastos, A. F., et al. (2025). “Academic-related stressors predict depressive symptoms in graduate students: A machine learning study.” _Behavioural Brain Research_, 478, Article 115328. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2024.115328](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2024.115328)

This publication provides context for the relevance of deadlines, performance concerns, finances, and academic progress. StudyBalance AI does not predict depressive symptoms and does not use its workload classes as a proxy for depression.

## Adaptive support

Kannan, J. V., Deepa, N., and Devi, T. (2025). “An adaptive reward-based behavioral model to optimize academic stress level in medical students.” _Discover Computing_, 28, Article 312. [https://doi.org/10.1007/s10791-025-09867-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10791-025-09867-w)

This work provides context for moving from prediction toward adaptive support. StudyBalance AI uses bounded deterministic planning rules, not federated learning or reinforcement learning, and claims no equivalent effectiveness.
